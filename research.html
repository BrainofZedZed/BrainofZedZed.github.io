Research

How experience organizes
memory circuits.

A memory can continue to guide behavior long after the experience that created it. I investigate how prefrontal circuits sustain that influence—and how it can change.

Current work / Memory consolidation

Recruiting neurons into
a lasting memory.

Neurons active during learning form only part of the ensemble engaged during remote recall. My work asks how additional cells become part of a lasting memory.

I identified prefrontal neurons projecting to the temporal association area (TEa) that are gradually recruited into a memory trace through the synaptic activity of learning-activated cells. Tracking the same neurons over weeks revealed stronger encoding of memory-related behavior. Inhibiting this pathway showed that it becomes necessary for remote recall.

Whole-brain mapping revealed prominent branches from the same neurons to the claustrum. Both TEa and claustrum show increased tone-evoked responses during consolidation, yet exhibit distinct response dynamics. Inhibiting the two branches has opposing effects on remote recall. These findings reveal a cell-type-specific mechanism of memory reorganization in which one prefrontal population distributes evolving signals across functionally distinct cortical targets.

Future directions

What determines persistence
and flexibility?

My future laboratory will connect local circuit mechanisms to distributed memory networks, treating circuit organization and experience history as determinants of how learned responses change.

01 / Recruitment

How do local circuits select memory pathways?

I will test how learning-activated neurons and local inhibitory populations regulate the recruitment of projection-defined prefrontal neurons as memories consolidate. Comparing fear conditioning, active avoidance, and stress-enhanced fear learning will reveal how behavioral control and prior trauma shape these recruitment rules.

Approach: longitudinal calcium imaging, activity-dependent labeling, cell-type-specific manipulations, and tests of pathway necessity.

02 / Coordination

How do branching pathways coordinate behavior?

Branches from the same prefrontal population can have different behavioral effects. I will identify the target cell types and downstream pathways that produce these differences, and test whether prefrontal input during consolidation helps establish a region’s later role in memory recall.

Approach: whole-brain circuit mapping, projection-specific recordings, and temporally targeted inhibition during consolidation and recall.

03 / Plasticity

When and how does enhanced plasticity support memory updating?

I will investigate how psychedelics and related plasticity-promoting compounds interact with established memory circuits and new learning. A central question is whether circuit organization at the time of treatment determines the consequences of induced plasticity.

Initial studies will examine tabernanthalog, a non-hallucinogenic psychedelic analogue, at different intervals after learning and around extinction training. This work aims to explain when and how induced plasticity supports adaptive updating, and which circuit mechanisms may instead broaden or sustain learned threat responses.

Approach: longitudinal imaging, projection-specific spine measurements, pathway manipulations, and behavioral models of persistent threat.

These studies address mechanisms relevant to persistent threat responses in PTSD, with longer-term extensions to addiction and depression.

Approach

Measurements that connect scales.

Track neural activity

Longitudinal calcium imaging follows identified neurons over time; fiber photometry measures population and neuromodulatory signals.

Identify & test pathways

Activity-dependent labeling, whole-brain clearing and imaging, and targeted circuit manipulations connect anatomy to function.

Analyze population activity

CEBRA-based low-dimensional embeddings and population decoding reveal how neuronal activity represents experience and behavior.

Build accessible tools

Open-source software and experimental hardware support reproducible behavioral measurement.
