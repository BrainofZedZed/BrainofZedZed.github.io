About

Zachary Zeidler, PhD

I’m a neuroscientist at UCLA investigating how prefrontal circuits reorganize to support lasting memories and guide behavior.

As a postdoctoral scholar with Laura DeNardo, I combine longitudinal neural recordings, circuit mapping, and targeted manipulations to study how memories mature. I also investigate how prefrontal dopamine supports learning to avoid threat.

During my PhD with Esther Krook-Magnuson at the University of Minnesota, I studied how cerebellar perturbations alter hippocampal activity and spatial memory, and developed a mouse model of ventral hippocampal epilepsy.

Across these projects, I work at the intersection of cellular mechanisms, distributed circuits, and behavior—with an emphasis on building experiments and tools that make those connections measurable.

Research expertise

I combine cellular and circuit measurements with behavioral analysis and disease-relevant animal models to connect memory mechanisms with translational questions about persistent learned responses and behavioral flexibility.

Track neural activity

Longitudinal calcium imaging follows identified neurons over time; fiber photometry measures population and neuromodulatory signals.

Identify & test pathways

Activity-dependent labeling, whole-brain clearing and imaging, and targeted circuit manipulations connect anatomy to function.

Analyze population activity

CEBRA-based low-dimensional embeddings and population decoding reveal how neuronal activity represents experience and behavior.

Build accessible tools

Open-source software and experimental hardware support reproducible behavioral measurement.

Methods include rodent behavioral assays and disease-relevant animal models, optogenetics and chemogenetics, miniscope calcium imaging, fiber photometry, in vivo electrophysiology, whole-brain clearing and imaging, CEBRA-based low-dimensional embeddings, population analysis, and neural decoding.

I have mentored undergraduate and graduate researchers and taught in UCLA’s Biology of Learning and Memory course and the University of Minnesota’s Human Neuroanatomy course.

My mentoring emphasizes clear scientific questions, rigorous experiments, and early opportunities to present and discuss research.

Scientific community

Supporting the people
who do the science.

I co-founded UCLA SYNCS (Seminars by Young Neuroscience Community Scholars) to expand opportunities for scientific exchange and highlight contributions to open-source tools and the broader community.

As chair of UCLA’s Postdoctoral Research Union, I advocated for more than 1,000 UCLA postdoctoral scholars and participated in negotiations that improved working conditions and secured childcare support. These gains benefited postdoctoral scholars across the University of California system.
